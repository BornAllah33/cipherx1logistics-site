Fake Meeting Survival Guide

Job interviews, client calls, investor meetings, podcast invites — scammers use all of them to get you to install something on your own computer. A fake podcast host just tried it on me. Here's how to spot it in seconds.

The 5 red flags

  1. The meeting link is on a look-alike domain. Real: huddle01.com, huddle01.app, zoom.us, meet.google.com. Fake: huddle01.site, zoom-us.site, meet.google.com.verify-call.site. Read the part right before the first /.
  2. "Your device isn't supported — use Windows or Mac." Real meeting apps run in any modern browser. Pushing you to a different computer often means their trap only works on that one.
  3. "Download and install this to join." A call never needs a file from the host's link. That download is the attack.
  4. "Paste this command to fix your audio / camera." If a page or a person tells you to paste something into Run, PowerShell or Terminal, it's taking over your computer.
  5. Pressure to record your screen, or to book only through their scheduling link. Your screen and camera can be reused to fool the next victim. A real recruiter, client or host is fine with your link.

The 3 safe moves

  • Make the link yourself. Open the official meeting site by typing its address, create the room, and send your link. A real recruiter, client or host joins. A scammer makes excuses.
  • Never install anything to join a call. Get apps only from the official site or your device's app store — never from a link someone sent you.
  • Never paste a command someone sent you. No real meeting, website or support agent needs you to.

If you already clicked

  1. Don't open anything you downloaded from the link, and don't run anything it asked for.
  2. Run a full virus scan (Windows Security, or your system's antivirus).
  3. If you typed a password on that page, change it from the real site or app — then turn on 2-step sign-in with an authenticator app or passkey.
  4. If you ran a file or a command: disconnect that computer from the internet, and from a different, clean device move your crypto to new wallets and change your important passwords.
  5. Report and block the account, and warn the company or person it claimed to be.
