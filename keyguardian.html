What It Protects You From

When a wallet, a trading pool, or an autonomous agent runs on its own, the key that controls it is the whole game. If an attacker slips in an unauthorized key or quietly lowers a signing threshold, they can drain the account before a human ever notices.

KeyGuardian stands watch over that exact moment. It continuously checks your account's on-chain authority, and the instant something is wrong it signs a new key and swaps the old one out — in under a second, approved by a quorum of your own co-signers and sealed by a hardware key you hold. The attacker is left holding a key that no longer works.

It never holds your keys. It never moves your funds. It only ever rotates your account to a key you control — on your machine.

How It Guards

🛡️Hardware root of trust

Every rotation is signed by your YubiKey — a physical touch. The signing key lives in hardware and never touches software. Nothing rotates without you.

👥M-of-N approval

No single compromise can rotate anything. A rotation needs a quorum of your co-signers to approve first — 2-of-3 by default, your call.

⚛️Post-quantum upgrade

Rotate an at-risk classical key straight to a post-quantum scheme (ML-DSA). The "record now, break later" threat is answered before it arrives.

🧾Tamper-evident record

Every decision is sealed into a SHA-256 hash chain. After any incident you can prove exactly what was seen and done — and that the record wasn't edited.

⏱️Clock-tamper proof

Moving a clock can't disarm it. A time anomaly freezes the guard and demands hardware re-authentication — it never quietly goes dark.

🦀Bare-metal, never panics

Zero-allocation Rust with checked arithmetic throughout. A guard that crashes is a guard that failed — so this one is built not to.
